For specialist suppliers

A tender might fit the fleet on paper. Then comes the detail.

A notice turns up with the right keywords. But the crew is assigned elsewhere, the vessel class is wrong, or the job is too far away to mobilize at the quoted rate. Those are the questions worth checking early.

01

The right work

Procurement portals often classify marine work broadly. A job can be missed because the search terms are too narrow.

02

The right equipment

A good-looking tender can require a class notation or clearance your fleet cannot provide.

03

The right price

Estimating time is expensive. We want fewer pointless bids and better questions on the serious ones.

How we would put the weekly list together

We start with public tender notices. We read for actual marine work, record the source and deadline, and flag the conditions that a contractor should check in the official documents. A request for information or an award gets a different label from a live tender.

This is independent research. The issuing authority and the full tender package are the final source of truth.
