FIELD NOTES / MARINE TENDERS

Nine things worth checking before you bid.

A marine tender can look straightforward until someone checks the certificates, the crew roster and the journey to the job. Here are nine places to look before the estimating hours add up.

A practical screening guide, not a substitute for professional technical or legal review.

GATE 01 / PASS OR FAIL

Have we met every mandatory condition?

Before pricing, check required site visits, previous contract experience, bidder registration, specific certifications, security clearances and supplier-eligibility restrictions. Some requirements are hard pass/fail conditions. A single missing bond or mandatory visit may be decisive.

Ask: Can we put the actual evidence in the bid package today?

GATE 02 / VESSEL CAPABILITY

Which vessel is actually doing the job?

Confirm the exact load line, class, flag, draft, bollard pull, deck load, ice class or DP capability stated in the procurement. Use the current certificate, not the fleet brochure.

Ask: Which named asset will perform the work, and is its proof valid throughout delivery?

GATE 03 / CREW

Who is going to crew it?

Transport Canada explains that safe manning documents specify minimum complement and certification requirements for affected Canadian vessels. Crew shortages, training timelines and watchkeeping restrictions can change the economics of a contract. Transport Canada guidance.

Ask: Can we staff every shift within the certificate and rest-hour requirements?

GATE 04 / MARINE LOGISTICS

What does it cost to get there?

Towage, transit fuel, pilotage, berthing, customs, support craft, crew changes and return voyage often sit outside the headline work scope. Four sites may mean four different mobilization plans.

Ask: Is every voyage, permit and port move in the price?

GATE 05 / SCHEDULE

How many workable days do we really have?

Seasonal fisheries windows, weather limits, tidal access and environmental restrictions can reduce workable days. If the contract includes a hard completion date and delay penalties, work out how many days you can actually lose.

Ask: How many working days are actually available under realistic conditions?

GATE 06 / COST MODEL

What happens when the costs move?

Fuel, steel, third-party services, unforeseen growth work, insurance and port fees can all move. A firm price without change control or adjustment mechanisms shifts the exposure to the supplier.

Ask: What happens to margin when costs rise or the scope changes?

GATE 07 / CONTRACT RISK

How bad could a claim get?

Check the liability cap, indemnities, insurance wording, termination rights and delay penalties together. Ask your broker and lawyer where the exposure sits.

Ask: What is our maximum realistic downside, and is it insured?

GATE 08 / WORKING CAPITAL

Can we finance the work until we get paid?

Mobilization and payroll come before some payment certificates. Retentions, milestone approvals, slow variations and long payment terms can leave a capable contractor financing the client.

Ask: How many payrolls can we fund before the first invoice clears?

GATE 09 / BID MECHANICS

Who is responsible for pressing Submit?

Confirm the submission portal, account activation, addenda acknowledgement, question deadline, document formats and time zone. Set a company deadline ahead of the official one and get someone else to check the files.

Ask: Who owns the final submission and amendment check?
