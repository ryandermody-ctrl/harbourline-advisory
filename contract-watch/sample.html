EDITORIAL PILOT / 02 NOVEMBER 2026

Three tenders. Three reasons to look twice.

These are three public procurement examples. For each one, we have listed the questions we would want answered before deciding whether to bid. They show the sort of research we are testing for the weekly email.

Prepared 2 November 2026. We have not checked every tender attachment or amendment. Dates and status can change. Open the official notice before taking action.

01 / Marine construction / British Columbia

DFO · 30008504Listed close: Nov 10, 2026

Fraser River dredging: can the equipment reach all four sites in time?

Public listing: propeller-wash and clamshell dredging at four Small Craft Harbour locations along the Fraser River. The published description identifies a fish-habitat work window of December 1, 2026 to February 15, 2027.

What we would check

  1. Can the equipment, berth access and crew move efficiently between four geographically distinct sites?
  2. Is the available working time compatible with the seasonal environmental window, weather restrictions and disposal requirements?
  3. Have the surveyed bottom conditions, dredge-volume assumptions and disposal logistics been verified from the specifications?
  4. Does the quoted price include weather standby, mobilization and any site-specific constraints?

THE QUESTION

Can all four sites be reached and worked in the permitted window?

Owning a dredger tells us very little about whether four locations can be covered safely and profitably inside a fixed work window.

02 / Ferry and port planning / British Columbia

038CS139344Listed close: Nov 23, 2026

Marine transport planning: the word “marine” is doing a lot of work.

A BC Ministry of Transportation and Transit procurement for marine transportation planning services. The underlying opportunity is professional and technical advisory work, not the charter of a ferry or vessel.

What we would check

  1. Can the company demonstrate planning, demand, operational analysis and business-case experience at the required standard?
  2. Does the procurement require specific named professional credentials or multi-disciplinary capacity?
  3. Are regional travel, consultation and technical deliverables costed accurately?
  4. Would teaming with a specialist materially improve eligibility or delivery quality?

THE QUESTION

This is planning work, not a vessel charter.

A vessel operator may find the title interesting, but the buyer appears to be seeking planning and advisory capability. That changes who should spend time on it.

03 / Vessel dry-dock refit / Eastern Canada

F5561-260373Listed close: Nov 24, 2026

CCGS Capt. Jacques Cartier: check who can bid before booking the dry dock.

Public listings describe a dry-docking and refit procurement. One published synopsis states that sourcing is restricted to suppliers in the vessel's Eastern Canadian area of origin under the relevant shipbuilding policy.

What we would check

  1. Does the bidder satisfy the geographic sourcing restrictions as stated in the current solicitation?
  2. Can a suitable dry dock be secured for the stated work window, including contingency?
  3. What is known work, growth work, and latent condition risk?
  4. Are buyer attribution and current amendments consistent across official notices and third-party listings?

THE QUESTION

Is the yard eligible to bid?

Before discussing dry-dock dates, check the geographic sourcing rule in the current solicitation.

Source confidence: Public listings disagree on contracting-organization attribution. Treat the original source as authoritative and investigate before making decisions.
